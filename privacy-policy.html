Privacy Policy

Last updated: October 20, 2026

Who we are and what this covers

PEAK ("we", "us") is a personal wellness and self-tracking app. This policy explains what information the app collects, why, who it is shared with, how long it is kept, and the choices you have. It covers the PEAK app and the PEAK support and legal pages. PEAK is a wellness tool, not a medical service, and is not a "covered entity" under HIPAA.

What we collect

Account information: your username (the name PEAK calls you) and email address, and your sign-in credentials, which are handled by Supabase authentication — PEAK never stores a password in plain text.

What you log: meals (the description, voice transcript, photo or barcode you provide, and the nutrition worked out from it), activities (including duration, intensity, distance, sets, reps and added weight), your daily mood rating and optional gratitude note, hydration, eating windows, weights, and the daily habits you mark done (the date and the habit's sentence).

Your profile and plans: height, sex, birthday, goals, Your PEAK Vision, milestone and weekly focus, your priorities, activity level, eating style, supplements you take, lifestyle selections, calorie, macro and step targets, which goal Home features, the meals you archive from Log again, and your app color. Also what you save or build inside the app: saved recipes, your Grocery List, meal plans and fitness plans, custom circuits, saved routes, notes, any experiments you start, and the short poem a Meditation guide session ends with. Older versions of PEAK also stored sleep and body check-ins and Daily Diary entries. Anything you entered there remains part of your export, is not sent to Claude or anyone else, and is deleted with your account.

Apple Health (optional): if you connect it, PEAK reads workouts (including distance and calories burned), sleep, steps, resting heart rate and heart-rate variability so they can appear alongside what you log by hand. PEAK only reads from Apple Health and never writes anything back. A small number of accounts connected Google Health before that option was retired for new connections; those existing connections keep syncing until disconnected in Profile, and no new ones can be started.

Precise location (optional, only while tracking): if you use Fitness > Route Tracker to record a Walk, Run, Hike or Bike ride, PEAK collects your precise location only while tracking is actively running, to compute distance, pace and the route. It is requested in the moment you start a route and never collected in the background. The route is stored on that one logged activity.

Voice input (optional): where you tap a microphone button, your speech is converted to text using your device's speech recognition. Depending on your device settings, Apple may process that audio under its own privacy terms. PEAK receives and stores only the resulting text, never the audio.

Notifications and device: if you allow notifications, your device's push token is stored so reminders can reach you. Reminders you schedule on your device stay on your device. Face ID or Touch ID, if you turn it on, is handled entirely by your device; PEAK never sees your biometric data and only keeps a sign-in token in your device's secure storage.

Kept only on your device: the Home cards you save and a record of which cards you have been shown, the habit Home suggests each day, your workout preferences (days a week, equipment and the kinds of exercise you enjoy), the other apps you ask PEAK to connect to, and your reminder settings. These are not stored in your account and are not part of an export. They stay on that phone and are removed when you delete the app. Your workout preferences are sent to Claude when you ask for a workout suggestion, as described below.

Technical information: crash reports and coarse usage signals, described under "Crash reports and app analytics" below.

Before you create an account

You can check in and set up your plan before signing up. To save what you enter, PEAK creates an anonymous account for you on the first screen. It holds no email and no name, only what you choose to give during setup: a mood check-in, a meal you describe and choose to log, your goal, and any body details, targets and goal date you enter. A meal you describe during setup is sent to Claude to work out its nutrition, as described below. If you go on to create a profile, that same account becomes yours and what you entered carries over. If you don't, it is left holding only those answers, and you can clear it from your phone by deleting the app.

Sharing in Connect

You can choose to share with other PEAK users: accountability partners (up to 5), coaches (up to 5) and communities such as a running club or workout group (up to 10, of up to 50 people each). You make an invite code in Connect > Share and give it to someone you choose, or you enter a code someone gave you. An invite can also be sent as a link, which opens a page on our website that shows the code and hands it to the app; that page collects nothing. Anyone who has an account and holds a code can use it, so share it only with people you trust. A coach can ask PEAK to verify their credential, described below; otherwise PEAK does not verify that a coach is qualified.

For each connection you choose what to share, from: your streak, weekly goals hit and habit days; this week's workouts, active minutes and recent sessions; calories against your target and this week's average; your recent tracked routes, with about 0.2 miles cut from each end so a route that starts and ends at home does not show where you live; progress toward your goal weight (pounds to go and the 30-day change, not your weight itself); and this week's average mood check-in. Calories are never shared with a community. In a community you can also join a weekly leaderboard, which shows your active days and active minutes this week. Your app writes a separate summary for each connection holding only what you chose for it, with your username, the date it was written and the last day you logged anything (so a connection can see you have been quiet, never what you logged). It is refreshed a few seconds after you log or change something. The people in that connection, and only they, can read it. They cannot see your meals, notes, weight, mood entries, health data or anything else, and the database does not allow it. A coach sees what you share and does not share back; partners and community members see each other's.

You can change what you share, leave a community or end a connection at any time from Connect > Share; access stops immediately, and the summaries you shared with that connection are deleted. A community's owner, or an admin the owner chooses, can remove a member. We cannot control what someone does with what they see, such as taking a screenshot, so share only with people you are comfortable sharing these figures with. Nothing is shared until you start a connection.

Within a connection you can also send preset cheers (such as “Nice work”), set a weekly challenge (a number of workouts, active minutes or active days, counted from what each person shares), and pause sharing with everyone at once; while sharing is paused, your connections see only that you have paused. A coach can send their client a short written note and suggest a weekly goal. The note is stored with that connection and only the coach and client can read it; a suggested goal is added to your account only if you accept it.

If you allow notifications, PEAK can send you a push when someone in a connection cheers you, reaches a streak milestone, finishes the week's challenge, or, from your coach, sends a note. These are sent from our server through Expo's push service and contain only the sender's username and fixed wording, never your data. You can turn them off for each connection in Connect > Share.

A coach can ask to be verified by submitting their full name, their credential, the body that issued it and, optionally, a credential or license number. We use it only to check the credential against the issuer's public register; only the coach and PEAK can see it. Their clients see only whether the coach is verified and the credential's name. Submitting again restarts the review, and the request is deleted with the account.

Where it lives

Your data is stored in your private, per-user account on Supabase (our database and authentication provider) and is protected by row-level security, so only you can read or write it, apart from the summaries you choose to share in Connect, described above. Data is encrypted in transit. No method of storage or transmission is completely secure, and we cannot guarantee absolute security. Our providers may process data in the United States and other countries.

How Claude is used

Several features send data through our secure backend to Anthropic's Claude API, which writes or interprets text for you. We never put our API key in the app.

Meals: when you describe or photograph a meal, that text or image is sent so the foods and portions can be identified. To save cost and speed up common entries, a short, generic meal description (for example "banana" or "black coffee") and the estimate returned for it may be kept in a shared cache that is not linked to any account, so another person typing the same words can reuse it. In the same way, the name of a food and the USDA record it was matched to may be kept in a shared library that is not linked to any account. Photos are never cached.

Guidance and insights: your daily Guidance (the "Today's guidance" reading, and the note that can appear on Home) is written from a short summary of your own figures — things like your streak, your average sleep and the names of the goals you have set — plus Your PEAK Vision and your priorities, if you have set them. Deepen your Gratitude is written from your recent gratitude notes. The note in "Your week in review" is written once a week from the same kind of figures.

On request: Ask PEAK sends the question you type along with the same kind of summary Guidance reads. The meal and workout suggestions on Diet and Fitness, the week planners, Chef's Kiss, Fitness Inspiration and the Recipe Generator use your goal, eating style or activity level, calorie or macro targets, priorities and Your PEAK Vision, your workout preferences, your recent activities, the names and ingredients of your saved recipes, and any ingredients or foods you type. Opening the recipe for a planned meal sends that meal's name and its planned calories, protein, carbs and fat. Importing a recipe from a link fetches that web page and may send its recipe text. Swapping an ingredient in Recipe Modifier sends that recipe's title, ingredients and instructions along with the ingredient you type; changing its servings is worked out on your phone and sends nothing. Nutrition Lookup sends the food name you type when it needs an estimate. The Meditation guide sends the options you choose during a session. A personal record on an activity can trigger a short congratulatory line built from the activity's name and the numbers involved.

Reminder messages: if you allow notifications, a nudge such as a streak reminder may be written by Claude from your current streak length and an internal identifier used to match the message to your device. It contains no health content.

Claude does not train on this data, and we do not use it to train models. Except for the shared meal cache above, we store the result that was produced for you (so it can be shown again) rather than a copy of what was sent. Your mood rating is a tap on a slider, not free text, and is never sent to Claude on its own. The free text PEAK sends to Claude is what you type or say for that purpose (a meal description, a question for Ask PEAK, ingredients, a food name or a recipe link), together with your recent gratitude notes and Your PEAK Vision. The gentle support message that can appear after a stretch of low mood ratings is generated entirely on your device.

Exact nutrition figures come from the USDA FoodData Central public database. Scanning a barcode looks the product up in Open Food Facts, a public food database; only the barcode number is sent.

Home articles and other written content

The cards on Home quote or summarize named sources — U.S. government health pages, public-domain books, and published studies, reviews and guidelines — and each shows where it comes from, with a link. Quotes are word for word. Summaries are drafted with AI assistance. The cards are stored in the app and chosen on your device, using your stated priorities and a record of which cards you have already been shown; neither is sent anywhere for this purpose.

The cards Home shows first, and every research summary, have been read one at a time against their sources in an AI-assisted editorial review for accuracy and safety. That is not a review by a licensed clinician. The cards are educational, are not written for you, and are not medical advice.

Home shows one small habit a day, taken from the library with no AI involved. If you tap Another, PEAK writes a different one: the area you are looking at (Diet, Fitness or Wellness) and the habits recently suggested on your device are sent to Claude to write it. Nothing from your logs is included. The suggestion is stored only on your device. When you mark a habit done, the date and the habit's sentence are saved to your account, so the record follows you to another device and can be listed in Connect; undoing it deletes that record. A habit marked done fills that day in the week's row of peaks on Home.

Tapping "Read the source" opens the publisher's website in your browser. That site is run by someone else and has its own privacy practices; PEAK does not send it any information about you.

Crash reports and app analytics

So we can fix bugs and understand which parts of PEAK people use, we rely on two service providers: Sentry for crash and error reporting, and PostHog for product analytics. They receive technical information — your device type and app version, the details of a crash when one happens, and coarse signals about how the app is used — together with your PEAK account identifier, so we can tell whether a problem hit one person or everyone. Those signals are things like which screen was opened, that a meal or activity was logged, which Home card was shown and for roughly how long, that a habit was marked done, the category and frequency of a goal you set (not its wording), your step goal when you reach it, the length of a streak milestone, a broad label for how often you have been logging lately, which Connect actions you take (such as sending a cheer or setting a challenge, never what anyone shares), and which other health apps you ask PEAK to connect to.

They never receive the content of what you log. No meal descriptions or photos, no mood ratings or notes, no sleep times, no goal wording, no weights, nothing about who you share with and nothing read from Apple Health is sent to either service.

Who we share information with

We share information only (1) with the service providers that make the app work — Supabase (database and sign-in), Anthropic (AI), Sentry (crash reports), PostHog (analytics), Expo (push notifications), and, for the public food lookups above, USDA FoodData Central and Open Food Facts; (2) with the partners, coaches and communities you connect with in Connect, limited to the summaries you choose for each; (3) when the law requires it, such as a valid legal request, or to protect the rights and safety of users or the public; and (4) if PEAK is ever acquired or merged, in which case this policy would continue to apply to your data unless you are told otherwise.

We do not sell your data. We do not share it for advertising. We do not show third-party ads, and none of the services above track you across other companies' apps or websites. Apple Health data is never used for advertising or sold.

How long we keep it

We keep your data for as long as your account exists so the app can work for you. When you delete your account, your data is permanently removed from our database. Backups and caches held by our providers may take a short additional time to clear. The shared food caches described above hold nothing linked to you, so they are not affected, and anything kept only on your device stays there until you delete the app.

Your control and your rights

You can export everything PEAK stores about you on its servers as a JSON file at any time from Profile > Data & privacy, including your connections and what you share with each. You can permanently delete your account and all associated data from the same place; deletion is immediate and irreversible. You can edit or delete individual logs inside the app, end a connection or change what you share, disconnect Apple Health, and turn any notification, Face ID or location permission off in the app or in your device settings.

Depending on where you live (for example California, the European Economic Area or the United Kingdom), you may have the right to access, correct, delete or receive a copy of your personal information, to object to or restrict certain processing, and to withdraw consent. Most of this you can do directly in the app; for anything else, contact us below and we will respond within the time the law requires. We do not discriminate against anyone for using these rights.

Children

PEAK is not intended for anyone under 16 (or the age of digital consent where you live). We do not knowingly collect information from children. If you believe a child has given us information, contact us and we will delete it.

Changes to this policy

If we make a material change, such as sharing a new kind of information or using it in a new way, we will update the date above and tell you in the app before it takes effect where the law requires.

Health disclaimer

PEAK is a wellness and self-tracking tool, not a medical device or a substitute for professional care. Calorie targets, burn estimates, nutrition figures, Guidance, insights and articles are general wellness information, not diagnosis or treatment, and may be inaccurate for you. Always consult a qualified professional before making significant health decisions. If you are in crisis, contact your local emergency services.
